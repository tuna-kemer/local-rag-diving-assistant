1T6 PHYSICS - PHYSIOLOGY

Water and Heat: Heat conducts through water by contact roughly 25 times faster than through air. Normal human body temperature is about 36.5 degrees C. Shivering begins when body temperature drops by about 1 degree.

About 50% of body heat loss occurs through the neck area, which is why divers wear hoods.

Water and Light: Only about 20% of sunlight penetrates to an average depth of 10 meters. Light travels at about 3/4 of its speed in water compared to air; as a result, divers perceive objects underwater as 25% closer and 33% larger. As depth increases, red, orange, and yellow colors fade to gray.

Underwater Sound: Sound travels about four times faster in water than in air (approximately 1550 meters per second in seawater). This makes it difficult for divers to determine the direction a sound is coming from underwater.

Respiratory System: Total lung capacity in an adult is about 6 liters. Normal resting respiratory rate is 12-14 breaths per minute.

Nitrogen Narcosis: Breathing air at high pressure has been observed to produce an alcohol-like narcotic effect, known as nitrogen narcosis or "rapture of the deep." Its effects are typically felt strongly starting around 30 meters.

Hypoxia and Hypothermia: Hypoxia is a condition in which body tissues do not receive enough oxygen. Hypothermia is an excessive drop in body temperature; loss of consciousness begins at 33 degrees C, and death can occur at 25 degrees C.
