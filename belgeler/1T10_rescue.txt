1T10 SELF-RESCUE AND RESCUE OF OTHERS

Proper Planning: The best way to prevent accidents is to have solid theoretical and practical knowledge through good dive training. The knowledge and experience of the dive group should match the planned dive.

Stop and Regain Control: If things are not going as planned, or if you feel uncomfortable or short of breath, stop and regain control. Signal the situation to your dive buddy and the dive leader.

Emergency Ascent Techniques: There are four types of emergency ascent. Buddy-breathing emergency ascent means sharing air by breathing alternately from a single regulator's second stage while ascending; it is only used when dive buddies are already very close together. Emergency ascent using an alternate air source means using an "octopus" (a spare second stage), and is the safest of the emergency ascent techniques. Controlled emergency swimming ascent is a self-reliant method; if not performed correctly, it carries a risk of lung injury and decompression sickness, and the regulator is not removed from the mouth. Buoyant emergency ascent carries a very high risk of lung injury, so positive-buoyancy emergency ascent techniques are taught only as theoretical knowledge and are not practiced.

Rescue Breathing: The LOOK, LISTEN, and FEEL technique is used to check breathing at the surface. Rescue breaths should be given at a rate of about 12-14 per minute.

Calling for Help: One-star divers may only dive with an experienced dive leader who holds at least a three-star certification. In the event of an accident, the victim must be brought to the surface immediately and help must be called.
