THE SEAS OF TURKEY

The Black Sea: Until around 6000 BCE, the Black Sea was a freshwater lake; after that date it merged with the Mediterranean and became a saltwater sea. It connects to the world's oceans through the Straits System in the southwest. The character of its surface waters is controlled by freshwater inflow. The Black Sea is the world's largest anoxic basin: because rivers deliver more organic matter than bacteria in the seawater can normally decompose using dissolved oxygen, these bacteria instead draw oxygen from sulfur ions in the water, producing hydrogen sulfide (H2S) gas, which prevents life below about 200 meters. The Black Sea is the world's largest reservoir of hydrogen sulfide, and there is no life below depths of roughly 150-200 meters.

The Black Sea has a positive water balance, since freshwater inflow (mostly from the Danube, Dnieper, and Dniester rivers, roughly 400 km3/year) exceeds evaporative water loss (roughly 350 km3/year). Its salinity is about 18 per mille.

Black Sea fisheries hold a special place among Turkey's living marine resources, accounting for about 82% of total fish production. In the 1980s, a comb jelly species, Mnemiopsis leidyi, was identified in the Black Sea; it consumed fish eggs and pelagic fish larvae, causing populations of commercially important fish such as anchovy (Engraulis encrasicholus) to decline. In October 1997, another comb jelly species, Beroe ovata, believed to originate from the northwest Atlantic, appeared and began feeding on Mnemiopsis, ending its dominance and greatly helping the ecosystem recover; the decline in the Mnemiopsis population led to an increase in non-gelatinous zooplankton, anchovy catches, and egg density.

The Sea of Marmara: The Sea of Marmara is an inland sea connecting the Black Sea to the Aegean and Mediterranean Seas. It connects to the Black Sea via the Istanbul Strait (Bosphorus) and to the Aegean Sea via the Dardanelles Strait, and it separates the Asian and European parts of Turkey. Hydrologically, the Sea of Marmara is a transition zone between the Black Sea and the Mediterranean; its structure resembles an estuary more than an open sea. Its surface water layer (50-75 m) reflects Black Sea characteristics in temperature and salinity, while the deep water layer below the thermocline reflects Mediterranean characteristics. Surface salinity can range between about 18-25 per mille depending on river input and rainfall, while the salinity of the lower water layer remains stable at around 38 per mille. Below the surface current carrying relatively low-salinity Black Sea water toward the Mediterranean, a bottom current flows in the opposite direction (west to east) as part of the region's salt balance. Heavy sea traffic, coastal development, fishing pressure, and domestic and industrial waste have reduced fishing yields in the Sea of Marmara.

The Aegean Sea: The Aegean Sea, also known as the Sea of Islands, lies between the Balkan and Anatolian peninsulas and connects to the Mediterranean. It is separated from the Sea of Marmara and the Black Sea by the Dardanelles and Bosphorus Straits; its northern boundary is defined by land, while its southern boundary is defined by the Greek islands of Rhodes and Crete. Nutrient-, oxygen-, and plankton-rich Black Sea water arriving through the Dardanelles positively affects fish life in the north. The Aegean Sea is rich in oxygen but poor in phosphate and nitrate, making its southern part one of the poorest seas in the world in terms of fish stocks. There are significant differences in temperature distribution and salinity between its northern and southern regions, dividing the Aegean into northern, central, and southern sub-regions; because of these differences, the Aegean is a meeting point for cold-water and warm-water species. It is biologically rich, home to roughly 400 species of algae, 5,000 invertebrate species, and 300 fish species. Despite this diversity, the narrow and complex coastline and seabed structure limit the areas suitable for trawling and similar fishing methods, so the Aegean accounts for only about 4-5% of Turkey's total fishing yield.

The Mediterranean Sea: The Mediterranean Sea connects to the Atlantic Ocean, bordered by Europe to the north, Africa to the south, and Asia to the east. It covers an area of about 2.5 million square kilometers and connects to the Atlantic through the Strait of Gibraltar.
