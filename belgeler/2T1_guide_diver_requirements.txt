GUIDE DIVER DEFINITION AND REQUIREMENTS

A guide diver is a certified diver who works within diving establishments to introduce, supervise, and protect the natural and cultural underwater values of the country for local and foreign divers, acting as a guide and supervisor.

Admission Requirements: To join guide diver training, a candidate must be a citizen of the country, hold at least a primary or middle school diploma, have completed at least six months since receiving the three-star diver certificate, have logged at least 50 verified dives documented in a dive log, meet the required health conditions, and know a foreign language designated by the Federation, passing the Federation's exam in it. Candidates must also have no disqualifying criminal record as defined by federation regulations, and must not have had their certificate revoked or have unpaid fines under diving equipment regulations.

Duties, Authority, and Responsibilities: A guide diver is a three-star diver (at minimum) who is registered with an authorized diving establishment, has attended the Federation's orientation seminar, and has completed the Guide Diver Course taught by a three-star instructor, passing the Federation's exam. On behalf of the diving establishment, the guide diver leads and supervises local and foreign divers underwater and/or at the surface for the purpose of introducing and protecting natural and cultural underwater values, and prevents damage to the underwater environment. The guide diver's presence is mandatory at every diving activity; they may be on the boat or in the water. If the establishment's regular guide cannot attend for a valid reason, another guide diver registered with a nearby establishment may temporarily be assigned, with the valid reason reported to the local authorities before the dive.

Guide Diver Exams: Guide diver exams are held as often as the Federation deems necessary. The content, location, date, and number of participants for the exam are determined by the Federation, and the schedule is announced at least one month in advance. Exams are conducted by an examination board of at least four people appointed by the Federation, and board instructors must hold a three-star diving instructor certificate.

Guide Diver Health Requirements: Because guide divers are responsible for the safety of other divers in addition to themselves, they must be in full health. Applicants for guide diver certification must obtain a report from an underwater medicine physician confirming there is no medical objection to diving, evaluated according to the equipment diving regulation. Health examinations must use the standard Health Examination Form, and the physician must issue a report using the standard Instructor-Guide/Diver Health Report form. Health reports are valid for five years. If a certified guide diver experiences a significant illness, whether related to diving or not, that could affect diving safety, they are required to report it and obtain a new report from an underwater medicine physician. Physicians who determine that a diver is permanently or temporarily unfit to dive must report this to the Federation; the diver's authorization is suspended accordingly and restored once a new report confirms fitness to dive. Health report decisions may be appealed to the Health Board, which may order additional tests or examinations before ruling.
